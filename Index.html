Electrical safety first
  • Switch off and unplug the appliance before touching or cleaning it.
  • Never open the casing, capacitors, or power boards. They can hold dangerous voltage even when unplugged.
  • Never work with wet hands or on a wet floor.
  • If you smell burning, see smoke or sparks, or feel a shock, switch off at the breaker and call a licensed technician.

🧰 Basic first-aid information

For emergencies, contact local emergency services immediately. These are basic safety reminders, not a substitute for first-aid training.

Minor cut or scrape

Wash your hands, rinse the wound with clean running water, apply gentle pressure with clean gauze if bleeding, and cover with a clean dressing. Get medical help for deep wounds, embedded objects, animal bites, or bleeding that will not stop.

Minor thermal burn

Cool the area under cool running water for 20 minutes. Remove nearby jewellery or loose clothing, but do not pull away anything stuck to the skin. Do not use ice, toothpaste, butter, or creams. Seek urgent care for large, deep, chemical, electrical, face, hand, genital, or major-joint burns.

Possible severe allergic reaction

Breathing difficulty, throat or tongue swelling, faintness, or rapidly worsening symptoms are an emergency. Call emergency services immediately. If the person has their prescribed adrenaline auto-injector, help them use it according to their training.

Possible stroke

Sudden face drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty, sudden vision loss, or sudden severe balance problems require emergency help immediately. Note the time symptoms began. Do not wait for symptoms to pass.

Possible heart attack

Chest pressure or pain, shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, or pain spreading to the arm, back, neck, or jaw can be an emergency. Call emergency services; do not drive yourself.

Emergency reminder: If someone is unconscious, having trouble breathing, experiencing severe chest pain, or showing stroke signs, seek emergency help now. Do not rely on an online guide.
